Before you pull up a chair...

Every one of us knows someone who once walked with God and now does not. A friend from school. A cousin. A colleague. Perhaps somebody we love who is still with us but whose faith quietly died somewhere along the way.

Almost every one of them left because a why was never answered. Not a philosophical why for the most part. A deeper one. A wound. A confusion. A question they were never given permission to voice inside a church. This evening the Table welcomes the guest most churches send away. Doubt. Not to enthrone it. To feed it. Because a well-fed doubt often becomes a stronger faith than one that was never allowed to speak.

Step 1 — Read
1
John 20:24–29

Thomas — the intellectual doubter.

He wanted evidence before he would believe. Jesus did not scold him. Jesus gave him exactly what he asked for. Then he sent him to India.

2
Mark 9:14–29

The father — the emotional doubter.

He said the words every honest believer has felt — I believe, help my unbelief. Jesus honoured the mixed heart and healed his son immediately.

3
Matthew 11:1–11

John the Baptist — doubting from prison.

The greatest prophet ever born of a woman sent messengers to ask are you really the one? Jesus did not rebuke him. He sent back scripture.

4
Habakkuk 1–3

The prophet who confronted God twice.

Habakkuk demanded answers about injustice. God answered him. And by chapter 3, Habakkuk wrote one of the most beautiful declarations of faith in scripture.

5
Job 1–2, 38, 42

The man God called "my servant" after every accusation.

Job argued with God for over thirty chapters. God's final verdict — Job spoke rightly. The theologically correct friends did not.

6
Jude 22 · Matthew 28:17

Two verses that change everything.

Jude says be merciful to those who doubt. Matthew records that at the Great Commission, some of the eleven still doubted. And Jesus commissioned them all anyway.

Step 3 — The Three Kinds of Why
Learning to Recognise the Real Question

Not every why is the same kind of why.

The Head Question

The Intellectual Why

How do we know God exists? How do we know the Bible is reliable? How do we know Jesus rose from the dead? These come from a mind trying to understand.

Needs: Evidence, scripture, reasoning
The Heart Question

The Emotional Why

Why did God allow my son to die? Why did my father leave? Why did I pray for years and nothing changed? These come from a heart in pain.

Needs: Presence, lament, the Psalms
The Will Question

The Moral Why

Why should I forgive her? Why should I stay pure? Why should I obey when nobody else is? These come from a will deciding whether to actually do the thing God is asking.

Needs: A vision of Jesus more compelling than the alternative
Step 4 — How Jesus Handled Doubters
The Master of Meeting People Where They Are

Three doubters. Three responses.

One consistent posture — Jesus drew closer to every one of them.

To Thomas — he gave evidence

Put your finger here. Reach out your hand. Jesus gave Thomas exactly what he asked for. No shame. No lecture. Then Thomas fell to his knees — my Lord and my God.

To the Father in Mark 9 — he gave action

The father said I believe, help my unbelief. Jesus did not require him to sort out his theology first. He healed the boy immediately. Sincerity was enough.

To John the Baptist — he gave scripture

John doubted from a prison cell. Jesus did not rescue him from prison. He sent scripture — Isaiah 35 and 61. Then he called John the greatest man ever born of a woman.

Step 5 — Four Questions to Carry Home
Where the Answers Actually Come From

Scripture, the Holy Spirit, and Sincere Wrestling

Four portable questions to bring to the Table when doubt sits down beside you.

1

Is this a head, heart, or will question?

Because the wrong kind of answer will not satisfy the right kind of question.

2

Am I asking for an answer, or for permission?

Sincerity is the door. God meets the honest seeker. He does not chase the person who has already decided.

3

Where in scripture has God already answered this?

Almost every honest human question has been asked before. The answer is somewhere in the text. Search for it.

4

What is the Helper saying to me as I sit with this?

The Spirit is Counsellor for the head, Comforter for the heart, and Spirit of Truth for the will. Sit still. Listen.

Step 6 — Journal
Question 1

"Have you ever watched someone you loved walk away from faith over a question that was never answered? What was the question they carried?"

Almost every one of us knows someone. Sit with the face that comes to mind.
Question 2

"Think of a why you are carrying right now. Is it a head question, a heart question, or a will question? And knowing that, does the answer suddenly need to look different?"

Getting this wrong is what makes so many good people walk away.
Question 3

"Am I asking my why because I want the answer, or because I want permission to leave?"

This is the sincerity question. Only you and the Helper know the true answer.
