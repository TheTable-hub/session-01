Before you pull up a chair...

Last month we discovered that the Table has become the Temple. The Helper lives inside every believer. This month we go one step further. The Temple becomes the fire. And we discover the one sacrifice God has been looking for all along.

We will meet two kings who both lost almost everything a man can lose. Same losses. Same magnitude. Same pain. But one ended his life destroyed. The other ended his life restored. The single difference between them is the most important lesson this session has to offer. Read the passages slowly this week.

Step 1 — Read
1
Psalm 51

David's prayer after Nathan.

The most famous prayer of repentance in the Bible. Read it slowly. Notice verse 11 — do not take your Holy Spirit from me. Ask yourself why David feared this above every other loss.

2
1 Samuel 15

The moment Saul lost the Spirit.

Saul kept the best animals to sacrifice to God instead of obeying the specific word. Samuel's response — to obey is better than sacrifice — is one of the most important sentences in the Old Testament.

3
1 Samuel 16:14–23

Where David saw it happen.

The Spirit departed from Saul. An evil spirit tormented him. And David was brought in to play the harp when the torment came. David had a front row seat to what losing the Spirit looks like.

4
Leviticus 6:8–13

The fire must be kept burning.

The instruction to the Old Testament priest — the fire on the altar must not go out. Every morning, fresh wood was added. Every single morning. This is now the pattern for the New Covenant Temple.

5
Acts 2:1–4

Fire in the Upper Room.

Tongues of fire rested on each of the disciples. The New Covenant was officially inaugurated inside every believer — visualised as fire. The same fire that walked between the pieces for Abram now burns inside you.

Step 2 — Fire at Every Covenant Moment
The Visible Signature of God's Covenant Presence

Fire has always been how God shows up.

Every major covenant moment in scripture is marked by fire. This is not decoration. It is signature. And it means the fire inside you today has been burning since Abram.

🔥

Genesis 15 · Abram

A smoking firepot and blazing torch pass between the pieces. God alone seals the covenant — as fire.

🌿

Exodus 3 · Moses

The burning bush. The covenant God comes down to rescue his people — speaking from fire.

☁️

Exodus 13 · The Wilderness

God leads his rescued people by day as cloud and by night as fire. The covenant God travels with his covenant people.

⛰️

Exodus 19 · Sinai

God descends on the mountain in fire and smoke. The Old Covenant is officially inaugurated.

🕯️

Leviticus 6 · The Altar

Fire from heaven falls. And the instruction is given — the fire must be kept burning. It must not go out.

⚡

1 Kings 18 · Carmel

Elijah calls down fire against the prophets of Baal. The covenant God alone answers by fire.

🕊️

Acts 2 · Pentecost

Tongues of fire rest on the disciples. The New Covenant is inaugurated inside every believer.

Step 3 — Two Kings, One Difference
The Central Comparison of This Session

Same losses. Different endings.

1 Samuel 15–31
Saul
Lost his son Jonathan. Lost his kingdom. Lost his family. Lost his sanity. Lost his life on Mount Gilboa.
Destroyed
2 Samuel
David
Lost his son Absalom. Lost his kingdom. Lost his family. Lost his wives to public humiliation. Lost his closest friend.
Restored
One offered a substitute. The other kept the fire.

Psalm 51 — David's Response

A prayer against becoming Saul

Verse 11 · The Centre

Do not cast me from your presence or take your Holy Spirit from me.

Verse 12

Verse 17 · The Sacrifice God Wants

The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise.

Step 5 — Six Practices for Keeping the Fire
The Daily Rhythm of a Temple That Refuses to Go Cold

Keep the Fire Burning

Six practices. One continuous rhythm. Start with one.

1

Feed the Fire Daily With the Word

The Old Covenant priest added fresh wood every morning. The New Covenant Temple must do the same with scripture. Ten minutes. Every day. Faithfully.

2

Search Me and Know Me

Psalm 139:23–24. Invite the Spirit to surface what does not belong. Because what you cannot surface, you cannot repent of.

3

Offer a Broken and Contrite Heart

Psalm 51:17. When the Spirit convicts, do not delay. Do not negotiate. Bow immediately. That reflex is what kept David's fire alive.

4

Live in a Posture of Thanksgiving

1 Thessalonians 5:16–18. Not thanksgiving for what happens. Thanksgiving in all circumstances. The atmosphere of a burning Temple.

5

Renew Your Mind Through the Word

Romans 12:1–2. The world pours in a pattern that will slowly extinguish the fire. The word is the counter-current. Give it enough time to actually renew.

6

Cooperate With the Spirit to Break Bondages

Hebrews 4:12. Every bondage has a corresponding truth in scripture. When the word goes in and the Spirit applies it, the chains begin to fall.

Step 6 — Journal
Question 1

"Have you ever watched someone lose the presence of God in their life? What did that teach you about your own walk?"

David watched Saul. That front row seat shaped every prayer he ever prayed afterward. Who have you watched?
Question 2

"Is there a specific word from God — through scripture, conscience, or a person he has put in your life — that you have been quietly offering a substitute for instead of obeying?"

Sacrifice looked spiritual for Saul. But it cost him everything. What are you offering as substitute?
Question 3

"Of the six practices above — which one is the Helper putting his finger on for you right now? And what one small thing can you do this week to add fresh wood to the fire?"

Start one. Faithfully. Every day. This is the question the whole session is building toward.
