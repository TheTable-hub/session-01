Before you pull up a chair...

Psalm 23 is perhaps the best known passage in the entire Bible. Many of us can recite it by heart. But there is something hidden in it that almost everyone misses — a moment where the Shepherd of the first four verses quietly becomes the Host of the last two. And in that moment, the entire identity of The Table finds its meaning.

Read the psalm slowly this week. Sit with each verse. Ask yourself honestly which Shepherd's voice you have been listening to — and whether you have actually sat down at the table he has already prepared for you.

Step 2 — A Discovery in Turkey
Hisardere · 2025 · Verified Archaeological Find

The earliest known portrait of Jesus is not on a cross.

In August 2025 archaeologists in the village of Hisardere — near the ancient city of Nicaea in Turkey — opened a sealed underground tomb that had not been disturbed for seventeen hundred years.

On the wall behind the burial platform they found a fresco. A young man in a simple tunic. Clean-shaven. Surrounded by goats. Carrying one of them across his shoulders. It was Jesus — depicted as the Good Shepherd.

The fresco dates to the early third century, painted during active persecution of Christians under the Roman Empire. Believers at that time could not paint crosses on their tombs — it would have invited execution. So they chose this image instead. The Shepherd carrying his sheep on his shoulders.

That is what they wanted to be the last thing visible over their beloved dead. Not a king. Not a warrior. A Shepherd carrying home the one that could not walk on its own.

Step 3 — The Arc of the Psalm
From Pasture to House — The Whole Christian Life in Six Verses
🌿 Pasture (Verses 1–3)

The Shepherd leads, provides, and restores. Already done. Already arranged. The sheep that is with the Shepherd lacks nothing.

🏔️ Valley (Verse 4)

Even through the darkest valley, the Shepherd does not leave. His rod and staff bring comfort. The journey passes through darkness — but never alone.

🪑 Table (Verse 5)

The Shepherd takes off his cloak and sits down as Host. The table is prepared. The oil of welcome is poured. The cup overflows. Even in the presence of enemies.

🏠 House (Verse 6)

Goodness and love follow all the days. And the journey ends in the eternal house of the Lord — the dwelling he himself has gone ahead to prepare.

Step 4 — The Heart of the Session
Verse 5 · Where Everything Changes

The Shepherd becomes the Host.

In verse 4 the Lord carries a rod and a staff — the tools of a shepherd. In verse 5 he prepares a table, pours oil, and fills a cup — the tools of a host. Same Lord. Two different roles. And the transition between them is the most important moment in the entire psalm.

This is why our gathering is called The Table. Not because it sounds warm. Because the entire architecture of our relationship with God moves toward a table — where Christ himself is the Host.

🪑

He Prepares It

The work of laying the meal is his. You do not bring anything you produced yourself. Your place is already set.

🛡️

Before Your Enemies

The Host does not wait for the threats to disappear. The table is set in the middle of the storm — and nothing can overturn it.

🫒

Oil of Welcome

In the ancient world, oil was poured on the head of an honoured guest. You are not tolerated at this table. You are celebrated.

🍷

The Cup Overflows

The Host does not measure your portion to what you have earned. He gives more than you can hold. The provision is extravagant.

Step 5 — Journal
Question 1

"Which voices in your life have been loudest lately — and have you tested any of them against the voice of the Good Shepherd in his word?"

The sheep follow the Shepherd because they know his voice. Which voice do you know best right now?
Question 2

"Have you been trying to live like an elephant — relying on your own memory and strength? Or are you willing to live like a sheep, trusting the Shepherd to remember what you cannot?"

Both answers are honest doors. There is no right answer. Just an honest one.
Question 3

"There is a table being prepared for you in the presence of your enemies. Right now. This week. Are you sitting at it — or standing nearby, waiting for permission?"

This is the question the whole session is moving toward. Sit with it honestly before you arrive on Sunday.
